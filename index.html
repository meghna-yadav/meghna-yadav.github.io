I am a PhD candidate in the Wilf Family Department of Politics at New York University. I study bureaucracy, state capacity and representation in India.

My research asks how the state comes to know the people it governs, and who decides what is done with that knowledge. One strand follows frontline bureaucrats: how long they stay in a posting, what local knowledge they accumulate while they are there, and how discretion over case assignment shapes the way rural development and forest rights programs are implemented. A second strand turns to the production of information itself, asking when local governments invest in making their residents legible to the levels of the state above them, and when they have little reason to. A third examines what follows from India's political quotas, where reservations for women and for scheduled castes and tribes can apply to the same seat.

Before starting my PhD, I was a Research Associate at the Social Networks Lab at J-PAL, where I worked on the effects of caste-based political reservations on attitudes and social networks in Bihar, and at the Centre for Development Economics at the Delhi School of Economics, where I studied barriers to the take-up of childcare services and their consequences for women's work. I have also worked on survey teams studying social security across several Indian states.

I hold an MA in Economics from the Delhi School of Economics and a BA (Honours) in Economics from Shri Ram College of Commerce, both at the University of Delhi.

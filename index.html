Meghna Yadav

Hello! I am a PhD candidate in the Wilf Family Department of Politics at New York University. I study the political economy of bureaucracy, information and identity with a regional focus on India.

My research asks how state personnel decide to learn about the people and places they govern, and how they utilise that knowledge. In my dissertation, I address three questions: how, and under what conditions, governments assign and rotate local versus outsider officers across territories; how those assignment decisions shape the knowledge officers acquire; and how that knowledge affects downstream accountability and policy implementation.

I study these questions in two contexts — the forest and rural development bureaucracies — combining original survey and experimental data with administrative records and qualitative fieldwork. I work with elite populations and like to think about creative low-cost ways of accessing them for research. My research has been supported by the NSF–APSA Doctoral Dissertation Research Improvement Grant and New York University’s Identities and Ideologies Research Grant.

I hold an MA in Economics from the Delhi School of Economics and a BA (Honours) in Economics from Shri Ram College of Commerce, both at the University of Delhi.
